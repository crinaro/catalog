Terms for the catalog

These terms cover pages under crinaro.ai/catalog/ only. They do not cover any other part of crinaro.ai.

Rendered 2026-10-29

Our reading, not the vendor's

Each line in the catalog that cites a page is our summary of that one page, published by a product's maker or by its documentation host, as we read it on the date shown beside the line. It is an opinion, offered to help a person or a team with a decision, and the reader is expected to confirm it on the vendor's page. The vendor's page is the authority on what the product does and on the vendor's own terms. A line is not a statement by the vendor.

The catalog is not legal, compliance, security or procurement advice.

Where a product runs, and where its models run, are given in the catalog's own placement words, and they are not our reading. For the product the words are: your machine; a container environment you run (containers on a server, or in a cloud account, that you control); a cloud service. For its models they are: your machine; a container environment you run; the model company's cloud; a cloud provider's model service. Each word shown rests on a sentence from the maker's own pages, which the page links and dates. A place that is not shown is one the pages we read do not mention, not one that is ruled out, and "not stated" or "not read" means no page we read said it or we have not yet read one. For a model in a cloud, the data and compliance lines to read are those of the company running it, and which of them covers a given use is for your organization to confirm.

The data and compliance lines on a product page are what the maker's own pages said on the dates shown. They are not a compliance determination: whether a product may handle your data is settled by the agreement your organization signs and by its own counsel. A mark followed by (company-wide) rests on a sentence that names no product, and a mark followed by plans in brackets, or by a condition, holds only there.

A line that reports nothing found on the pages we read lists those pages, and it means exactly that. It never means the vendor does not offer the feature.

Our own readings

Some product pages carry a section headed "Our reading, beyond the maker's pages". Each item in it is our own view of how a product compares or groups with other products in the catalog, or of a limit worth knowing about. It is not a summary of any one page and links none. It is not a statement by the product's maker, or by the maker of any other product it names, and no maker has reviewed it. An item does not say where a product runs or where its models run; the catalog gives those only as lines that cite the maker's pages. Where an item names another product, it places the two side by side and says nothing about that other product beyond what that product's own page in the catalog reports. Each item shows the date its view was first written. Rewording an item keeps that date; changing what the item says gives it a new one. A second reviewer, an AI model separate from the one that wrote it, read it before it was published. Nothing records a person reading it. An item cannot be confirmed on a vendor's page; if you think one is wrong, write to report@crinaro.ai and say which item and why.

Corrections

To report an error or ask for a correction, write to report@crinaro.ai . Send the page you are looking at, the line you think is wrong, and the vendor's page that shows it. For an item under "Our reading", there may be no such page: say which item you mean and why you think it is wrong. We correct or remove a reading once we have read what you send, and a corrected reading carries its new date. The address is read by people. We use what you send to correct the catalog and to improve how it is made, and we do not publish your message or your name. A correction is made once we have confirmed it against the vendor's page, and the corrected line carries its new date. We make no promise about how quickly we reply.

How the catalog is made

The catalog is made with language models under a person's direction. Nothing records a person reading any item. A language model drafts each summary of a page, and each item in a product page's "Our reading" section. A second reviewer, an AI model separate from the one that drafted it, reads each item before it is published, reading a page summary against its page, and an item that reviewer has not read is not shown. Two kinds of item are the exception: setup links (the "How to set it up" links to a vendor's guide) and short requirement phrases (such as the hardware a product needs). An automated screen checks them. The second reviewer reads every item the screen flags and a random 20% sample of the items it passes. A passed item outside the sample is published with no reviewer having read it. Any item can be wrong, and these have had only the screen. We accept that risk. If you find one, tell us (see Corrections). An item whose text changes after the screen is held until it is checked again. A line is a summary of the page in the catalog's words, which may follow the page's wording closely, and the page is the authority.

Every line that cites a page links it and shows the date we read it. No line or reading repeats six or more consecutive words of a vendor sentence we recorded, and the build refuses one that does. Wording on a vendor's page that we did not record is not checked. Shorter overlaps, such as a product's name or a setting's name, can occur.

The catalog covers 159 products. Each page shows the date its own content last changed.

114 lines were left out of this build, each counted once under the first rule that refused it: 36 flagged for a person to read; 25 held until a re-read, because the page changed since it was read; 19 repeating six or more consecutive words of a vendor's sentence; 15 citing a site we have not yet classified for license terms; 8 stating a negative without saying whose page says it; 4 held back until a second reviewer has read the wording; 2 about a discontinued product; 2 duplicate; 2 carrying markup or a link in its wording; 1 using a verdict word in our own voice. None was left out for age. A second reviewer has read the wording of every summary line published. Of the setup links and requirement phrases, 88 passed an automated screen and fell outside the random sample the second reviewer read. They are published with no reviewer having read them. Our own readings held back from this build: none.

Dates and expiry

Every line shows the date we read its page, and a page may have changed since. A line is built with a 90-day limit: a line whose page was read more than 90 days before a build is left out of that build. The catalog is rebuilt by hand, not on a schedule, so a published line can be older than 90 days between builds; the date beside each line says how old it is.

A reading shows the date its view was first written, and the same 90-day limit runs from that date: a reading whose date is more than 90 days before a build is left out of that build, even if it was reworded since.
