An environment is the controlled place work runs: who signs in, what the network allows, which services are approved, who changed what. You have one for each product. AI assistants now let almost anyone build a working page in minutes, and they take in whatever data a person hands them. That work happens on each person's own laptop, outside any environment. Two costs follow. The work stays with the person who made it, and customer data goes wherever the assistant sends it.

The work stops with the person who made it

An analyst asks her AI assistant for a page that shows renewals by region. She gets one in minutes. A viewer can click a region, filter by month and drill into the accounts behind each number. Her team wants it.

The tools she has for sharing were built for documents and slides. So she pastes a picture of the page into a deck and emails it. Her colleagues see a flat copy. They cannot click, filter or drill, and they cannot build on it. Next quarter someone asks an assistant to build a similar page from scratch.

That is the business cost: work paid for once and built again. It is our experience of the usual sharing tools, not a survey of them.

The data goes wherever the assistant sends it

The same people hold customer data, and their assistants take it in just as readily.

  • A support engineer copies a customer's settings and records into her assistant to reproduce a fault. She gets an answer in minutes. The assistant sent the records to the AI service it was set up to use, an online service that runs the model, and one the company approved for code. Nobody checked whether that service may hold customer records.
  • A service rep pastes a tangled account history into a free AI tool on his own login. The company does not know he uses it.
  • A seller uploads a customer's contract to a tool from a link a colleague shared. Nobody on the security team has heard of it.

Each was trying to help a customer. Afterwards, the company may be unable to say which service got the data, whether it kept it, or whether the contract with that customer allowed it.

These stories are illustrative, drawn from what we and our peers have seen: testimony, not measurement.

It happens most where the controls are least understood

In our experience this sits less in engineering, where people understand why the controls exist, and more in support, customer service and sales, where they do not see why the controls are needed. Peers describe the same. We have not measured how often it happens or where.

What this means for you

If you answer for customers, contracts or compliance, you answer for where customer data went, and today that is decided one person and one assistant at a time. If you run a team or a business unit, what your people build stops with them, so speed gained by one person does not add up.

One question is worth forwarding to your security lead today: can anyone reach customer data in a production system from a laptop? If the answer is yes, the next pages are about you.
